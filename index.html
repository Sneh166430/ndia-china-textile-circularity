Research Overview

This independent research project examines textile waste management and circular economy initiatives in India and China. It aims to identify evidence-based opportunities for more sustainable apparel supply chains and small businesses.

Research Evidence & Preliminary Findings

This section summarizes selected findings from government publications on textile waste and circular economy initiatives in India and China.

India: Waste Recovery

India's Ministry of Textiles reported in March 2026 that over 70% of textile waste is recovered through recycling, reuse, upcycling or downcycling pathways.

Official Source - March 2026

India: Circular Economy Initiatives

Government initiatives include Tex-Eco, In-Tex India and research support for sustainable textile technologies.

Official Source - August 2026

China: Textile Recycling Targets

China's 2022 guideline set textile waste recycling targets of 25% by 2025 and 30% by 2030. These are policy targets, not confirmed achievement rates.

Government Policy Source - April 2022

Preliminary Research Interpretation

The reviewed sources indicate that both countries have identified textile circularity as an important area for policy and industry development. India's published evidence emphasizes waste recovery networks and circularity initiatives, while China's 2022 guideline establishes numerical recycling targets and system improvements.

These findings do not establish which country performs better. Recovery and recycling rates use different definitions, and policy targets must not be compared directly with observed outcomes.

Implications for Apparel SMEs

Potential areas for further research include fabric waste segregation, reuse of cutting scraps, material traceability, and affordable recycling partnerships for small apparel enterprises.

Research status: Preliminary secondary evidence synthesis. Further dataset verification and academic literature review are required.
